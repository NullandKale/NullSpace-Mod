# Habs: generation

Status: **the shells, layouts, rooms, powered doors, depth ramp, stock, logs and trees are built; the hab controller, its power network and placing habs in the world are not.** Written November 17. What a hab is for, and why, is in [habs and what space is for](habs-and-rewards.md#habs); this page is how one is generated and how it is reviewed. The work is split in [the work plan](rewards-work-plan.md#track-b-habs), and this page covers the first piece of it (B1) up to the shell.

**Decided** marks what the owner decided. Everything else on this page is a reading of those decisions that I chose so the shell could be built and looked at; each is listed again under [open points](#open-points) so it can be corrected.

## What a hab is

**Decided.** A hab is a station in the ground. Floor 1 is at the surface and the hab goes down from there; it is basically an inverted station. Generated habs are dungeons with good loot, harder than a station of the same size, and each holds one golden apple tree on its deepest floor.

## How it is generated

A hab is planned by the station planner, so it gets what a station gets: straight carriers, rooms flush along them in mirrored bays, a door in the middle of each wall, a tree of doors, and one tall arrival hall. [`HabPlanner`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java) runs the same layout methods ([`StationMethods`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationMethods.java)) and the same room programs ([`StationProfiles`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationProfiles.java)) as [`StationPlanner`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationPlanner.java), with these differences:

- **The entry hall is the arrival hall, and only its top story is at the surface.** The hall is as tall as the whole hab and its top story is floor 1. **Floors are joined by one ladder shaft, not stairs:** every level of the hall is a full floor, with a one-cell shaft in a corner on the climb's wall and a single ladder climbing through it from the lowest floor to the surface hall ([`StationStructure.ladderShaft`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationStructure.java)). Each floor's arms leave the hall by their own doors, so the room graph is still a tree. Every arm and room is on a level below it, so the surface shows one small building and the rest is underground. The lowest level is the biggest floor, which is a station turned over.
- **Everything is larger.** There is more space underground, so halls, bays and rooms are four blocks wider and deeper than a station's, the corridors are seven wide instead of five, and the entry hall is at least 15 across.
- **Each arm is a wing with a purpose.** The arms leave the entry hall in four directions and each is one of four wings ([`HabPlanner.Wing`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java)): the **living wing** (cabins, bunks, the commons, kitchen, infirmary), the **works** (workshop, foundry, stores, maintenance, cargo, engineering, EVA prep), the **gardens** (growing bays and gardens, with the grove at the end of its deepest floor) and the **command wing** (labs, archive, observation, operations, armory, brig, lookout). A hab's purpose picks which wing leads and the seed picks the order of the other three. Each arm is dealt only its own wing's rooms, on every floor, and what a wing has no room for goes wherever there is room left, so a wing is at least 80 percent one kind of life. A Bastion hab, a single chain of halls, runs its wings one after another along the chain. **Wing size is optional** ([`HabPlanner.scalesFor`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java)): about a third of habs have four arms of the same length; the rest are lopsided on purpose, with the purpose's wing at full length and each other arm between 40 and 100 percent of it, so wings can be shorter or longer. A shorter arm asks for proportionally fewer rooms, which keeps its wing pure at the cost of a hab with fewer rooms overall. The first 26 blocks of each arm are left bare so crossed arms clear each other's deep rooms. Each hall door to an arm has a sign (in the gallery) naming its wing and floor.
- **Habs are the biggest air sections supported, up to 512 by 512.** A station is built in a 128 by 32 by 128 box; a hab is not. Its arms leave the entry hall in all four directions, so it fills a square and not a strip, and it asks for more rooms than will fit and keeps as many as do. With the arms of different lengths a small hab averages about 175 by 95 blocks and 40 rooms, a medium one about 280 by 180 and 120 rooms, a large one about 450 by 290 and 225 rooms, and a large one can reach 512 across. Every hab keeps the required rooms of its purpose (a large one asks for those of a medium station).
- **No berths.** Nothing docks at a hab, so the plan is not faulted for having none ([`StationPlan.problems(false)`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationPlan.java)).
- **A grove at the end of the deepest floor.** A big garden hall **two levels tall**, 23 to 35 blocks across, stands at the free end of a corridor on the lowest level, the biggest size that fits. The arms are laid out inside a margin of 23 blocks so there is always room past an arm's end for one. It is furnished by its own design, the **central tree** ([`RoomsLiving.grove`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/RoomsLiving.java)): one tree on the middle line in a bed of podzol ringed in stone brick, paths from the door and across the hall and round the tree in a square, flower beds along the way in, a lantern on a post at each corner of the ring, small trees in the corners (ponds in a wide hall) and a frame of a golden apple on the back wall. The tree is a real [golden apple tree](#trees).
- **Habs only take purposes that need no docks.** A harbor or cargo brief becomes a habitation; habitation, agriculture, industry, research and security stay.

| Size | Floors (hall story = floor 1) | Arms reach into a square of | Rooms asked for | Grove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | 3 | 192 blocks | 110 | 27 or 23 across |
| Medium | 4 | 320 blocks | 270 | 35, 31, 27 or 23 |
| Large | 4 | 512 blocks | 560 | 43 down to 23 |

A taller hab would pass the 32-block height limit of a plan, so large habs are wider and not deeper. The three makers (Rivet, Halcyon, Bastion) give the palette and the layout shape, as they do for stations.

## The shell

[`HabStructure`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabStructure.java) builds the blocks with the station passes ([`StationStructure`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationStructure.java)), told where the ground is:

- **Buried.** Nothing under the ground is glazed or roofed, since rock is there; the surface story keeps its windows and its roof.
- **A door at the surface.** An iron door with a pressure plate each side, in the entry hall's top story, in the wall at the foot of the last flight, clear of the stairs.
- **Cameras and viewports.** Four cameras look out of the surface hall, one from the middle of each wall (the one on the door wall is over the door), and one is high on the back wall of the grove looking down on the tree ([`StationStructure.cameras`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationStructure.java)). A station gets a camera over each berth's port and one on an outer wall of each viewing room (observation, lookout and operations), when the cell outside is inside the 128 by 32 by 128 box a station is built in. A monitor that nobody has set shows the **nearest camera** ([`ViewportDefaults`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/scene/ViewportDefaults.java)), so a viewing room's monitor wall shows the camera on its own outer wall and a monitor deep in a hab shows the closest one. A monitor that has been set keeps its choice.
- **Carved.** The build lists every room cell its blocks leave to be air, so placing it into terrain digs the rooms out.
- **The tree's spot.** A `tree` point at the middle of the grove's floor, for the tree to be placed by when it exists.
- **Plan coordinates.** The surface is the row `groundY`, the top block of the terrain, level with the entry hall's top-story floor, so a build is placed with that row at the terrain height.

The Station Core stands in the entry hall as the station's does. It is a stand-in: the hab controller replaces it when that block exists.

## Reviewing it

`/nullspace shipyard habs` builds [`HabGallery`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/shipyard/HabGallery.java) with nothing more to type: five medium habs side by side going east, one in each layout form (arms, beads, suites, stacked and ring), each dug into the land with level ground up to its surface door and a sign by it naming its layout, with their crew in them. Inside, a sign by each hall door names the wing it leads to and the floor; the door panels open only while a generator in the machine hall has coal; the chests are stocked by floor; the tree has one to three apples. The older arguments still work and are only shortcuts: `habs small|medium|large` builds the three habs of a size, `habs <size> <form>` builds all three in one form, and `live` after either adds the crew. A large hab is up to 512 blocks across, so the gallery is built a slice at a time over many ticks and takes you there when it is ready. Seeds are fixed, so a visit shows the same habs. Walk in the iron door and down the ladder.

It is checked by [`HabPlannerTest`](../../src/test/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlannerTest.java) (270 plans over every maker, size and purpose are sound; the surface holds only the entry hall; the grove is a big two-level garden on the deepest floor and no required room is lost; halls and corridors are larger than a station's; the same brief gives the same hab) and [`HabGalleryGameTest`](../../src/gametest/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/shipyard/HabGalleryGameTest.java) (every hab has its surface door, no windows under the ground, a tree trunk at the centre of a big two-level grove, a walkway from the door to the grove on the deepest floor that climbs the ladder, one unbroken ladder through every floor, five cameras, and rooms lit to block light 8 by the real light engine, including after the chunks unload and load again). [`HabGalleryClientGameTest`](../../src/gametest/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/shipyard/HabGalleryClientGameTest.java) takes pictures of the first one for review. A passing test does not say a hab is good to walk through; the owner's review of the gallery is what does.

## Layout forms

The seed gives each hab one of five layouts ([`HabPlanner.Form`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java), chosen by [`formFor`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java): Rivet leans to arms, Bastion to suites, Halcyon to rings, and a small hab never has plazas or a ring). All have the same tall entry hall, the same four wings and the same ladder shaft, so the structure, lighting and cameras do not change. The design is in [hab rooms and layouts](hab-rooms.md).

- **Arms.** A corridor out from the hall on every level, with rooms either side. The first 26 blocks of each arm are bare so crossed arms clear each other's rooms.
- **Stacked.** The same arms, but the east and west arms take the even levels and the north and south ones the odd, so crossing arms never share a level and none needs a bare lead: rooms start 6 blocks from the hall ([`StationMethods.hubForm`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationMethods.java)).
- **Beads.** A short passage to a plaza, 31 long and 23 across, with two rooms across from each other in two places round it, and the chain goes on through the plaza's far wall ([`beads`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationMethods.java)). On the levels where an arm's crossing neighbour has its plaza the first passage is 24 long to clear it; on the others it is 6, so on every arm one level in two has no long passage.
- **Suites.** Rooms that open into each other in a straight line from the hall, each attached to the far wall of the last, with a room beside it on each side from the third on ([`suites`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationMethods.java)). The rooms are the route, so a room with two doors keeps a three-wide walkway clear between them ([`RoomKit.lane`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/RoomKit.java)).
- **Ring.** A square of seven-wide halls round a courtyard with the grove in it, rooms along both sides of every hall, one wing to a side, and the entry hall outside the south hall; every level has its own ring ([`ring`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationMethods.java)). The loop is closed by a door that power holds shut, so without power the halls are a chain and with it a ring.

## Doors that only power opens

A **door panel** ([`HabDoors`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/habs/HabDoors.java)) stands where a stone pressure plate would, on each side of a door, and works like one (stepping on it powers the iron door beside it) but only while a generator of the hab has fuel. Each panel remembers the generators of its hab in its block entity, filled in when the hab is placed ([`HabDoors.link`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/habs/HabDoors.java)), so a door works from anywhere in the hab; it remembers whether it last saw fuel, for the times the machine hall's chunk is not loaded. Opening a door burns a little of the generator's fuel. Three kinds of door are powered ([`HabPlanner.poweredDoors`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java)): the controller room's, the entry hall's to the command wing, and a ring's closing door. **The machine hall is never behind one** ([`HabPlanner.problems`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java) rejects a plan that puts it there), so the way in is always: find the machine hall, feed its generators coal, and the doors open. Generators burn only for the doors for now: air supplies and lights in a hab do not draw on them until the hab controller and its power network are built.

## The depth ramp, the crew and the stock

[`HabDepth`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/habs/HabDepth.java) turns a floor into a danger: the lobby (floor 1) is safe and each floor below it is danger 1, 2 and 3 (a small hab, with two floors below the lobby, reaches 2).

- **Crew.** A room has a chance of one lost crew member, repair bot or (deeper) pest by its floor's danger, a dormitory has an occupant now and then (never a row), and the controller room has one guard for each danger level, the hard rank among them from danger 2. `HabDepth.plan` decides this from the plan alone and `HabDepth.populate` places it. The gallery leaves the crew out unless asked (`... live`).
- **Stock.** Each room's marked chest takes the room's table at the tier of its floor, so deeper chests give better stock, and the controller room's chest is the treasure of its floor's danger. The hab's own rooms have tables ([`generate_room_loot.py`](../../scripts/art/generate_room_loot.py)); the rooms of a hab's electronics hold the power-failure stock, since a hab starts without power.
- **Logs.** A lectern in each family's quarters, the chapel, the machine hall and the controller room holds a written book ([`HabLogs`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/habs/HabLogs.java)) that tells part of the outpost's story.

## Rooms and furnishings

A hab's rooms are the station designs for their use where a hab has no design of its own, and its own rooms where it has ([`RoomsHab`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/RoomsHab.java), each in the [room catalog](hab-room-catalog.md)). A hab's program turns homes into family quarters, the engineering room into the machine hall, a lookout into an observation room and a receiving bay into stores, and always has **one controller room and one machine hall**, and at most one chapel and one mine head ([`HabPlanner.program`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabPlanner.java)).

- **Family quarters.** Units of one or two beds with a chest at the pillow, a toy corner in some, a partition between units, a keepsake in a frame on the wall and a lectern and shelves at the back.
- **Chapel.** Benches facing a stone altar with a soul lantern, a wall of name tags in frames, one for each of the crew, and a lectern.
- **Machine hall.** The real generators, each with its battery, down both walls and across the back, two consoles, fuel barrels, pipes overhead and a lectern. The generators start with no fuel. One hall in three has failed with a leak (cracked floor and webs).
- **Mine head.** A square opening in the floor with a rail round three sides, a track and a lamp. On the deepest floor it opens on a **real cave**: a three-wide shaft with a ladder down one wall, a chamber under it and a smaller one off each side, and veins of iron, copper and coal in the walls ([`HabStructure`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabStructure.java)). On any other floor the opening has a grating over a shaft that goes nowhere.
- **Apiary** (the compost and bee room). Hives on both walls, flower beds either side of a path, composters across the back and two bees.
- **Controller room.** A raised dais of polished blackstone at the back with a console and screen on it (standing in for the hab controller until that block exists), steps up its front, a wall of screens behind and a lectern. The objective of the dungeon.
- **Camera wall** (the operations room). A giant wall of screens across the back and a desk of consoles facing it. The screens show the nearest camera until someone chooses one; cameras hang in the machine hall, the controller room, the commons, the apiary, every plaza and the grove, besides the four on the surface hall ([`StationStructure.cameras`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/StationStructure.java)).
- **Grove gallery** (the observation room). Benches facing a giant wall of screens, which shows the nearest camera (the grove's, deep in the hab), and plants down the sides.
- **Quarantine ward** (the deepest infirmaries, half of them). Each bed sealed in a cell of iron bars with no door, cobwebs in the corners and the ward's supplies in chests in the aisle.
- **Ore store** (a hab's cargo hold). Piles of iron, copper and coal ore down one side beside a track, double chests down the other, and a hopper and chest at the back.
- **The dormitory** is the station's bunk room; its occupants are the crew of the depth ramp, a few at most.
- **The surface lobby** ([`HabFurnishings.entryHall`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabFurnishings.java)): lockers (barrels in groups of three with a gap) along each side wall, stopping short of the ladder, a suit on a stand in each gap, and a potted fern in each corner by the door. The cells in front of the door and the ladder stay clear.
- **Stores follow their wing.** Spares in the works, seed in the gardens and general elsewhere.
- **The grove** is furnished by its own design and holds the golden apple tree ([above](#how-it-is-generated), [trees](#trees)).

The entities of the rooms (frames, suits, animals, logs), the stock, the panels' generators and (with `live`) the crew are placed by the gallery as a placed hab will have them. Not built: the hab controller itself, the power network the controller would own (air and lights drawing on the generators), and placing habs in the world. [`HabFurnishingsGameTest`](../../src/gametest/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/stations/HabFurnishingsGameTest.java) checks the lobby, the stores variants, each of the new rooms, the mine head's cave and that the way through a passed-through room is clear; [`HabSystemsGameTest`](../../src/gametest/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/habs/HabSystemsGameTest.java) checks the door panels, the depth ramp, the stock tables, the logs, the camera wall, gallery, ward and ore store, and the short passage of the beads.

## Trees

The golden apple tree and the enchanted golden apple tree ([`GoldenTrees`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/trees/GoldenTrees.java)) work the same way:

- **Shape.** A trunk core at the foot (the block that remembers the tree), five oak logs above it, and a rounded crown of leaves that never decay ([`TreeShape`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/trees/TreeShape.java)). Eight cells under the crown's lowest leaves can each hold a hanging apple: a small apple block on a stem, hanging from the bottom of the leaf above it.
- **Apples.** One apple for every Minecraft day, up to eight, counted from the game time the tree last looked, so a tree in a hab nobody is in has them when the hab loads again ([`TreeGrowth`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/trees/TreeGrowth.java), [`TreeBlockEntity`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/trees/TreeBlockEntity.java)). A full tree banks no time, so picking one starts the next day then. An apple is picked by hand (right-click) or punched off, and gives a golden apple from the plain tree or an enchanted golden apple from the enchanted one. A punched or picked apple leaves the tree's count, and an apple falls if its leaf is removed.
- **Saplings.** Breaking the trunk core drops **one sapling of its own kind**; leaves drop nothing. A sapling grows into its tree with light and room, as a vanilla sapling does. Destroying a tree gives one sapling, so a tree can be moved and never multiplied.
- **Only the golden apple tree is generated**, one in each hab's grove. **An enchanted tree comes only from nine golden apple saplings in a 3 by 3 craft**, one from each of nine hab groves.
- **Air.** A tree breathes out beside its foot like an Air Supply at normal power and needs no power ([`AirSystem`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/systems/AirSystem.java)), so a sealed hab with a tree has air of its own. The enchanted tree does the same. How much air one tree gives a whole hab is not tuned.
- **Looking at them.** The materials gallery has both trees with all their apples ([`MaterialsGallery`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/shipyard/MaterialsGallery.java)); every hab's grove has the golden apple tree.

## A hab is a large station

A placed hab is a registered station vessel ([HabVessels](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/habs/HabVessels.java)), so its Station Core in the controller room is the real thing: it shows the hab's power, fuel, air, leaks and doors, and the hab's machines run on the ship systems like a station's. A hab is given no free fuel; a generator needs coal. The gallery registers every hab it builds, and the station gallery registers each piece that has a Station Core.

A hab can be 512 blocks across, so it is a **large station**: its air grid is its whole footprint, up to 512 by 64 by 512 ([Atmosphere.Grid](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/model/air/Atmosphere.java); an ordinary station stays at 128 by 32 by 128). A large station is simulated from its own records and not from the chunks round it:

- **Seals** are worked out once and saved with the vessel; a block change updates the cells round it ([AirSystem](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/systems/AirSystem.java)). The air is worked out again from them without reading the world.
- **Air and seals are saved as runs** ([AirStore](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/gameplay/systems/AirStore.java)), written when the world is saved, since a dense grid of that size is tens of megabytes.
- **Its systems rebuild at most every two seconds**, and its device list is found again every five; a vessel runs when a player is near it and its controller's chunk is loaded.
- **Machines in unloaded chunks are off.** Generators, supplies and doors there count again when their chunk loads.
- **Its own blocks are recorded** (a list saved beside the world), because flooding outward from the controller through the rock round a buried hab would take in the whole hill.

Registering a hab takes about half a second at the largest size. (It once took minutes: the list of a hab's blocks was an immutable set, and block positions hash so badly that building one of two hundred thousand blocks probed almost linearly.)

Not yet tested: a large hab running with the chunks far from the player unloaded. The gallery test world has every chunk loaded.

## Not built yet

- **The explosions-and-air rules for sealed rooms** ([air and explosions](habs-and-rewards.md#air-and-explosions)); the rest of [the room catalog](hab-room-catalog.md) is built.
- **Placing habs in the world.** Frequency about as often as villages, finding a flat enough site (or fitting the entry hall to the terrain), planets with and without air, and the Terra, Nether and End cases.
- **Turrets and the green fluid by floor.** The crew and stock follow the floor ([the depth ramp](#the-depth-ramp-the-crew-and-the-stock)); the security turrets and green fluid leaks of [`SiteDefences`](../../src/main/java/com/nullandkale/nullspace/worldgen/encounters/SiteDefences.java) apply to a site as a whole today and would need to follow the floor.
- **The hab controller, the tree's state on its screen, player-built habs.** B2, B4 and B5 of the work plan. A tree that has lost its air or light does not yet stop growing, the trees are not in the field guide, and a hab is not yet required to have one.

## Open points

1. **Only the entry hall at the surface.** The reading is a small building on top with everything below; the alternative is a surface deck as wide as the hab, which looks like a station standing on the ground.
2. **Floors and widths** in the table above. They follow the station sizes and the 32-block limit.
3. **The grove is its own room at a corridor's end.** It is not one of the program's rooms, so it adds to the hab and no other room is lost; it needs the free end of a corridor on the deepest floor.
4. **Windows only at the surface.** Underground rooms have none; a skylight shaft or a lit garden dome for the grove is possible and not decided.
