# 0.5.2 rc1: changes and playtest checklist

This covers the changes from **0.5.1 to [0.5.2 rc1](https://github.com/NullandKale/NullSpace-Mod/releases/tag/v0.5.2_rc1)**, built from `2cdd4b0`. The JAR is named `nullspace-0.5.2_rc1.jar`; the in-game mod version is `0.5.2`.

This is a bug-fix and refactor release. Use the checklist to check whether the fixes work in normal play and whether the revised instructions make sense. An automated pass does not settle whether a screen is clear or an encounter is enjoyable.

## Changelog

### Travel, encounters and docking

- Signals are less frequent: about one in four eligible journeys, with a five-minute cooldown per vessel after a signal is announced. A small run of trips will not necessarily match that ratio.
- Unowned generated salvage can accept docking without powered controls on the target. Your departing ship still needs its own power and fuel. Owned targets keep their power requirements.
- **Trip** holds **Visit signal (optional)** and **Continue journey**. Ignoring a signal keeps the trip going. Continue journey automatically undocks and resumes the paid journey without charging jump fuel again.
- Trip text distinguishes preparation, view synchronization, cruising, signals, docking, securing the connection, departure, arrival waiting for safe crew, and completion. The last action is shown separately from current trip status.
- Engage waits for a destination's construction save instead of immediately trying to use an unfinished berth. Docking collars retain their protection while their shared save finishes; boarding explains why it is waiting.
- Interior doors can stay open during travel. Engage closes exterior exits; hull sealing and safe crew positions still matter. **Close all doors** remains available explicitly.

### Planets and landing

- All nongas planet bodies are landable; the old fixed surface pool no longer limits availability. Newly assigned surfaces have a stable dimension identity and their own terrain seed.
- Existing planet assignments, terrain and builds are preserved. Terra still uses the Overworld. Saved charts gain missing surfaces without needing a world reset.
- Toxic worlds use barren terrain and have no breathable air. Ocean and jungle surfaces are breathable; desert, ice, lava and barren surfaces are airless.
- Dropped items no longer block landing. They move outside the arriving hull rather than being deleted.
- Water and lava can give way to an arriving ship. Solid obstructions and living occupants still block unsafe placement. Lava remains a takeoff obstruction and a hazard after landing.
- Interrupted landing recovery also restores displaced fluids and scheduled ticks.

### Ships, supplies and ownership

- Deploying a stored ship consumes its matching carried titles after the move succeeds. Repeated store/deploy cycles should not accumulate titles. A refused deployment keeps its title.
- Newly stocked generated containers no longer receive default custom names that stop their dropped blocks stacking. Deliberate player names are preserved.
- New salvage vessels share eight coal across their generators instead of receiving a stack in each. Fuel rolls in supply containers are smaller too. Starter and purchased-ship provisioning is unchanged.
- Claim feedback and the guide explain that your previous ship stays where you left it. Claiming does not repair the target, refill it or automatically scrap your first ship.
- Battery help explains electrical reserves, discharge limits and the separate need for propulsion fuel. This is not a new fuel economy.

Existing supplies and names in already generated vessels are not rewritten. Check the supply changes on newly generated salvage.

### Air, screens and guidance

- An occupant partly inside a closed door or hatch reads air from the adjoining room. The door remains a seal; this does not create airflow through it or add oxygen.
- Healthy cabin air leaves the action bar quiet. Low oxygen, weak room air, recovery and suit/canister use have more actionable status text. Creative and spectator play do not show these survival warnings.
- The Scanner has one destination browser. Its upgrade screen links back with **Open scanner**, rather than presenting a second destination list.
- A refused or unconfirmed selection can be retried in the Scanner without visiting the Ship Controller to unstick it.
- Supported visible living actors, including encounter enemies and their equipment, are included in remote vessel views. Normal visibility and hull occlusion still apply.
- Passive mounted-monitor viewing extends from 12 to 32 blocks. The nearest-two-feed limit remains; this does not extend interactive controller reach.
- The Field Guide follows player actions and prerequisites, explains salvage/storage and manual hops, and orders recipes around progression. It includes the equipment and upgrade recipes missing from the earlier sequence.

### Saving and recovery

- Ship data now follows world saves and uses two alternating store copies. A readable older copy can be brought forward using committed journals when the newest copy is damaged.
- Physical moves use **MOVE** journals; construction/removal uses **BUILD** journals; ownership and other discrete metadata changes use **RECORD** journals.
- Coverage includes deliveries, station cores/modules and regeneration, docking collars, storage/scrapping, claims/titles, crew, respawn bindings and rescue bookkeeping. Older saved delivery orders retain a compatibility path.
- Completed BUILD operations share a save and keep their reservations until it finishes. Urgent first-arrival and rescue operations can force an immediate barrier. A brief construction or connection-saving state can therefore be expected.
- Unfinished operations recover or remain reserved and visible for operator attention. Bad or unsupported journals are contained and retained instead of crashing recovery. Finished journals are pruned once both store copies cover them.
- Controllers carry ship identity for chunk-load consistency checks. Disagreements are reported for recovery rather than silently assigning the wrong ship.
- The pad registry keeps a previous copy for startup fallback. That fallback can lose the most recent registration, which may need registering again.
- Recovery commands include inspection, retry, export, discard, and restoring a readable MOVE snapshot to a clear pad or free space slot.
- The save allowance is larger but remains bounded. Save, session, rendering, computer, power and ship-scan code also received internal refactors; their existing player behavior needs regression coverage.

BUILD/startup measurement tools and additional automated regression/crash checks were added. Further performance measurement and tuning are deferred. This release does not claim a measured speedup or guarantee a sub-second save pause.

## Before playing

- Use the rc1 JAR and matching dependencies. Everyone joining the same world needs the matching mod version.
- Start with an existing world if you have one: preserving existing ships, pads, destinations and builds is part of the change. Use a separate fresh world for the first-start and guide checks. Do not reset a world to make missing planets appear.
- Note your starting ship, destination, cargo, fuel, title count and any planet builds you will revisit. Take a few before/after screenshots.
- Bring normal travel supplies and suit air for airless destinations. Two players are useful for the crew checks, but most of this pass works alone.
- Mark an unavailable encounter type **not seen** and continue. Do not spend the session farming signals or running benchmarks.

## Main playthrough

Work through these in one play session where possible. Collect all issues before changing the build or repeating the whole checklist.

### 1. Guide, Scanner and first departure

- [ ] Read **Already on a ship** or **Build my first ship**, depending on your start. Follow the steps without filling gaps from memory. Note any missing prerequisite or unclear click.
- [ ] Use the recipe index and ingredient links to find the basic ship parts, air equipment, suit, canister and upgrades. Check that the recipe order matches what you need next.
- [ ] Open a physical Scanner, select a destination, open its upgrades and return through **Open scanner**. There should be one destination browser.
- [ ] Choose something out of range and try the relevant travel action. Then select a valid destination in the same Scanner session. **Set destination** must remain usable without fixing the selection at another block. Selecting a destination alone must not start travel.
- [ ] Engage with an interior door open. Interior airflow paths should remain open; exterior exits should close. A real exterior leak must still prevent unsafe departure and give useful feedback.
- [ ] Check launch, FTL entry and arrival text. During cruise, walk around, use storage and craft. Arrival should be automatic when the crew are safe.

The short view-synchronization fade remains. FTL entry and exit still use the space dimension and physical storage location; seamless space transitions are not part of rc1.

### 2. One signal visit, one signal ignored

- [ ] Ignore a signal on one trip. The trip should continue and arrive without a special stop/resume action.
- [ ] On another eligible trip, use **Trip → Visit signal (optional)**. The encounter should be selected and docking should start automatically.
- [ ] Read the approach, capture and connection-saving states. Wait for the connection to finish, open the dock door and board. You should not need another Engage to start the approach.
- [ ] Inspect supplies and, where windows or camera angles permit, look for enemies inside the other vessel. They should be visible in the remote view, not through opaque hull walls.
- [ ] Take an item and return aboard. Use **Continue journey** once. It should back away, undock and resume the original paid trip, without manual Undock or another jump fee. Ordinary generation and docking can still consume fuel.
- [ ] Revisit supplies while the site remains available. Loot must not refill. Do not expect an unclaimed signal to remain permanently after the journey ends.

Record which types you actually encountered:

| Growing station | | | | |
| Damaged freighter | | | | |

| Drifting cargo pod | | | | |

Pay particular attention to the cargo pod: its lack of target power should not make docking impossible. Judge difficulty and rewards separately from whether the encounter can be completed.

### 3. Land outside Sol, then come back

- [ ] Visit and land on nongas bodies in at least two systems outside Sol. Use intermediate hops when range requires them. Gas giants remain orbit-only.
- [ ] Revisit an old destination and confirm any existing build is still there.
- [ ] Visit two separate planets of the same terrain type. Place different small markers at matching coordinates, then revisit both. Their terrain and edits must stay independent.
- [ ] Try landing with a dropped item in the arrival footprint. The ship should land, and the item should remain outside its hull. Do not use a player or animal as the obstruction for this check.
- [ ] On a lava world, check arrival where lava would previously block placement. After arrival, lava must still be hazardous and may prevent takeoff until the obstruction is cleared. Look for damage, trapped crew or a broken pad.
- [ ] Stop and reopen the world normally, then revisit the marked planets and their pads. Destinations, terrain, markers and landing registrations must persist.

### 4. Air and electrical reserves

- [ ] In a healthy cabin, check that the oxygen text settles down instead of constantly covering the screen.
- [ ] Stand partly in a closed doorway. Your oxygen should follow the adjoining room rather than treating that block as vacuum. Try a framed hatch too if convenient.
- [ ] Open an interior connection between rooms with different air levels. Air should redistribute. Closing it should separate the rooms again; the closed-door fix must not act as a leak or an air source.
- [ ] Check low room air, suit/canister use outside, and recovery after returning to air. Warnings should tell you what to do, and clear when the problem is resolved.
- [ ] Charge a battery, reduce generation and watch a small electrical load run from stored charge. Charge should fall. A battery's discharge limit may not support every machine at once.
- [ ] With charged batteries but no propulsion fuel, confirm that jump fuel is still required. The equipment help and guide should explain why.

### 5. Loot, titles and claiming a vessel

- [ ] Count fuel across both containers and generators on newly generated salvage. Note vessel size/type and whether supplies still feel excessive or too scarce. Previously generated stocks are not a valid balance comparison.
- [ ] Empty and break several newly generated containers of the same block type. Their block items should stack. A container deliberately named by a player should keep that name.
- [ ] Store and deploy your ship three times. Each successful deployment should consume its matching carried titles; storing it again should leave one usable title, not another accumulating copy. Check cargo and damage each time.
- [ ] Try a deployment that is refused because its destination is occupied. The stored ship and title must remain available for a valid retry.
- [ ] Claim a claimable unowned derelict. Read the instructions: you should know which vessel you now own, where your original ship remains, and how to return to or recover either one. Claiming must not refill loot, repair the target or remove your original ship.

### 6. Monitors and general regression

- [ ] View a mounted monitor from beyond 12 blocks, within 32. Its feed should remain usable. With several monitors nearby, only the nearest two feeds are expected to run; interactive controls still require normal reach.
- [ ] Check a camera feed, a window view, manual flight and a planetary sky. Look for missing ships, wrong rotations, enemies in the wrong place, blank views or persistent transition overlays.
- [ ] Purchase or deliver a vessel, then leave and return. Construction should finish once, retain its supplies and not leave permanent reservations.
- [ ] If you use stations, build a core/module and try expansion. Wait for construction/save completion before the next operation. Verify a usable dock, room air and boarding in both directions. Your edits must survive revisiting.
- [ ] Check a ship bed/respawn, a normal rescue, and computer files if these are part of your play. After travel and a normal world reopen, bindings, carried inventory, ship cargo and saved computer contents should still agree with what happened.
- [ ] Note obvious freezes during ordinary play, including delivery or docking, but keep this pass to normal gameplay. No large-ship benchmark or pause target is required.

### 7. Two-player checks, when available

- [ ] One player pilots or uses the Controller while another opens the guide, Scanner, systems or a camera. One person's screen must not replace the other's session or change a destination without an explicit selection.
- [ ] Have a crew member log out aboard, travel with the remaining crew, then have them rejoin. Check location, inventory, suit, crew membership and respawn binding.
- [ ] Let everyone leave a cruising vessel offline, then rejoin. An empty vessel should not have completed the journey while nobody was aboard.
- [ ] Build or deliver at one vessel while another crew uses an unrelated vessel. Construction reservations should stay local to the affected work.
- [ ] If testing title handover, replacement or scrapping, check that old titles lose their authority at the right time and that the result persists after reopening the world. Use expendable assets for scrapping.

## Optional recovery checks

These already have automated crash/restart coverage. They are for an operator using a **disposable world copy**, not the normal playthrough. Keep an untouched backup. Do not corrupt files or force a crash in a world you intend to keep playing.

- [ ] Upgrade a copy of an existing 0.5.1 world, reopen it and check ships, ownership, cargo, pads, planet builds and stored vessels. Existing identities must remain stable.
- [ ] Exercise interrupted MOVE and BUILD recovery in the copy. Check hulls, containers, entities, passengers, offline arrivals and records together. Recovery must not duplicate a vessel or overwrite unrelated work.
- [ ] Exercise a damaged newest store copy with a readable older copy and journals. Recovery should reconcile records with the saved world. If both copies are unreadable, refusal to load is preferable to silently starting with an empty ship store.
- [ ] Exercise one unreadable or unsupported journal. It should be retained and reported without aborting unrelated recovery. Readable unresolved operations should retain their reservations.
- [ ] Exercise pad-registry fallback. The previous registry should load; its last missing registration may need registering again. A failed manual reload must not erase the currently loaded registry.

Commands available for investigating an actual unresolved operation:

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `/nullspace recovery list` | Lists operations needing attention and damaged store copies reported at startup. |
| `/nullspace recovery show <id>` | Shows the selected entry and readable journal details. |
| `/nullspace recovery retry <id>` | Retries recovery after its cause has been addressed. |
| `/nullspace recovery restore <id> <pad name>` | Restores a readable current-format, single-vessel MOVE snapshot to a clear registered pad that fits it. `id:<pad-id>` also selects a pad. |
| `/nullspace recovery restore <id> space` | Restores that MOVE snapshot to a free space slot. |
| `/nullspace recovery export <id>` | Exports readable recovery data for inspection or manual rebuilding. |
| `/nullspace recovery discard <id>` | Accepts the world as it is and releases the entry's locks; it does not restore the ship. |

Use IDs from `list`; unique prefixes are accepted. Restore is not a general undo command and does not accept every BUILD, RECORD or unreadable journal. A refused or occupied destination must leave the original recovery data intact. Successful restoration should preserve the vessel, relevant entities and passenger recovery without leaving a second authoritative copy.

See [journals and save recovery](../design/journals-and-save-recovery.md) for the underlying behavior.

## Still deferred

Do not expect these in rc1: the two-room/tiny starter family, planetary habs, plant oxygen consumption or low-oxygen uprooting, sleeping through FTL, a NullSpace reset command, automatic multi-hop trips, seamless space transitions, the FTL jolt, new airflow/thruster particles, an arrival bell, or a redesigned fuel economy. The existing starter has not been made smaller. Further performance measurement and tuning also remain deferred.

## Scope and existing verification

The release comparison is [0.5.1…0.5.2 rc1](https://github.com/NullandKale/NullSpace-Mod/compare/v0.5.1...v0.5.2_rc1). The checklist covers the changed gameplay and persistence paths, plus regressions for the internal refactors. Source-formatting changes, documentation links and development-only measurement/video tools do not add player features or require separate play sessions.

Local acceptance covers 611 unit cases, 270 native cases with affected corrections, 31 selected graphical checks, multiplayer, 16 MOVE and ten BUILD crash/restart checkpoints, planet persistence and asset checks. [Release CI](https://github.com/NullandKale/NullSpace-Mod/actions/runs/43263207272) also passed. These are completed checks; this document is for your playtest, not a request to rerun the automated suites.
